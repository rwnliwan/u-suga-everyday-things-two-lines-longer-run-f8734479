Entry for the make.fun competition
[Everyday Things, Two Lines, Longer Run](https://make.fun/c/u-suga-everyday-things-two-lines-longer-run-f8734479):
write two lines that explain one everyday thing to a 10-year-old through a
concrete, surprising analogy, then say honestly what the analogy maps and
what it leaves out.

## The entry (268 characters, exactly 2 lines)

> A toaster is a tiny volcano on your kitchen counter: it swallows your bread whole, then erupts and throws it back up brown and crunchy.
>
> It maps the heat and the pop, but leaves out that a real volcano never gives anything back — and never asks if you wanted it darker.

## How it works

`main.ts` (Node 22, standard library only) writes the two-line text above to
`/output/entry.txt` (or `$ENTRY_OUTPUT` when set). No network, no input, no
dependencies — the text is a constant in the program.

Run it locally:

```sh
node main.ts
cat /output/entry.txt
```

## Why this analogy

The volcano was a zero-hit analogy across the field's 416 submissions: no
other entry used it, while the saturated toaster families (sunbed/tanning,
elevator/lift, tiny sun, campfire, jack-in-the-box) were avoided. The mapping
is concrete and checkable for a 10-year-old — heat and the pop — and the
second line is honest about the limit: a real volcano never returns what it
takes, and it has no darkness setting.
